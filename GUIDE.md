# 🐙 Tentacalendar — what it is and how to use it

Somebody sent you a link and didn't explain. Here's the whole thing in about ten minutes.

---

## What this is

A planning board that decides **what you should do next**, instead of letting you decide.

That sounds like a small difference and it is the entire point. Most to-do apps let you drag things around until the list looks comfortable. This one won't. You give it deadlines; it orders your day from them. If something is late, it goes to the top and stays there whether or not you feel like looking at it.

It is not a calendar, though it reads yours. It is not a project manager, though it runs projects. It is closest to: *the list you would write every morning if you were honest and had perfect memory.*

**It is also opinionated,** and you'll get on better if you know that up front. Several things you might expect to be able to change are deliberately fixed. Where that's true below, it says so and says why.

---

## Getting in

Sign in with Google. There's no account to create and no invitation needed — the first time you arrive, the app builds you a board of your own with three starter tiers (**Home**, **Work**, **Personal**).

That board is yours. Nobody can see it unless you hand them a key, and you can't see anyone else's the same way.

## The five things you need to know

### 1. Tiers

A **tier** is a bucket for the parts of your life — Home, Work, a specific class, a side project. Everything you create lives in one.

Tiers do three jobs:

- **They colour-code the queue** so you can see at a glance whether today is mostly work or mostly home.
- **They can be hidden.** Click a tier's chip to drop it out of the queue entirely. Useful on a Saturday when the work tier is just noise.
- **They order your day when nothing else does** — see the queue rules below.

You can set which **days of the week** a tier is active. A tier set to Mon–Fri vanishes from the queue at weekends, which is the polite way to stop your job following you home.

Two kinds of tier:

| Kind | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Tasks** | Things you create and check off. The normal one. |
| **Calendar** | Appointments pulled in from a Google Calendar. Read-only, they pin near their start time, and they never nag. |

**Ordering tiers:** ▲▼ arrows in ⚙️ ▸ Tiers. You can't type a number — position *is* the number. If a tier is shared with someone, **you each order it your own way**: the tier is shared, your priorities aren't.

### 2. Tasks

A title, a tier, and a due date and time. That's it to get started.

**You can type any date** instead of picking it: *10/15*, *Oct 15*, *fri*, *tomorrow*, *+2w* (two weeks from today) all work, and so does *1015* on a number pad with no slash. The 📅 beside the box opens the calendar if you'd rather tap. If the box turns red, it couldn't read what you typed — nothing saves until it can.

Two optional things worth learning, because they're where the app earns its keep:

**Escalation — *"If ignored, nag again every ___."*** Set a task to nag every day, or every hour, or every six months. When the deadline passes, the task turns red and climbs; on each escalation step it climbs again. A thing set to nag hourly becomes genuinely hard to ignore by lunchtime. A thing set to nag every three months sits quietly and resurfaces in the spring.

This is the feature to reach for when you catch yourself thinking *I'll remember.* You won't. Set the nag.

**Repeats — *"↻ Repeats every ___."*** Recurring tasks come in two flavours, and the difference matters:

- **From completion** — the next one appears when you finish this one. Good for *change the water filter*: the clock starts when you actually did it.
- **After scheduled due** — the next one appears on schedule regardless. Good for *rent*: it's due on the 1st whether or not you paid on time.

### 3. The queue, and why you can't reorder it

The middle of the screen is your queue. It sorts like this, in order:

1. **Anything expired**, oldest deadline first.
2. **Everything else chronologically by deadline.** *Tier does not matter here* — a home thing due at 2pm beats a work thing due at 4pm.
3. **Projects that need attention**, by how far through them you are.
4. **Tier order**, only to break a tie.
5. Alphabetically, if it's still a tie.

**There is no drag-and-drop, on purpose.** A list you can rearrange is a list you can rearrange until the frightening thing is at the bottom. If something is in the wrong place here, the fix is to change its deadline — which is an honest act, because you're admitting the date moved.

### 4. Projects and stages

A **project** is something with a start, an end, and steps in between — *run the school play*, *close the quarter*.

Steps are called **stages**, and each one is anchored to the project's start or end with an offset: *three weeks before the end*, *the day it starts*. Set the project's dates and every stage's deadline computes itself. Move the end date and they all move with it.

Stages then enter your queue like tasks, at their own deadlines. You don't have to think about the project at all; you just do the next thing when it turns up.

**Pipelines** (⚙️ ▸ Pipeline) are saved stage templates, so a project you run every year comes with its steps already in it. New boards start with **no template** — that's deliberate, not a bug. Yours should be yours.

One stage per project can be marked the **hurrah** — the moment the thing actually happens, as opposed to the paperwork afterwards. Finishing it gets you a bigger celebration than finishing the last stage, because opening night matters more than filing the receipts.

**Steps that fall outside the project's dates** — send the engagement letter two weeks before it starts, invoice a week after it ends — leave the pipeline and become ordinary tasks, so a project doesn't clog your list before it has begun or after it's done. Open a project's stages (✎⋮) or expand its card and they're listed underneath as **Tasks from this project**, so you can still review the whole thing in one place.

**A step *after the end* waits for the real end.** Once the project is under way it sits in **Waiting on…** saying *"5 working days after this project is finished"*, and only when you actually finish — the hurrah, if it has one — does it get its date. Publish a week late and the invoice moves a week with you, without you touching it. After you finish, it stays in Waiting on… with its date (*"due Thu, Oct 1 — in 9 days"*) so you don't forget it, and moves into your list on the day.

**Projects that haven't started yet keep their follow-ups to themselves** — next year's reports don't clutter Waiting on… for nine months. Look for them on the project's own card.

**Handy things on a project:**

- **▸ on a project's row in Today** opens its whole pipeline right there. Tick any stage, in any order — the row keeps showing the next one that's still open.
- **📋 Duplicate.** A copy with its checkmarks reset — and the "Move the dates" buttons (+1 week, +1 month, +1 year…) shift both dates in one tap. It remembers which one you used for that project, so laundry comes back at +2 weeks and work comes back at +1 year. The before/after steps come along too.
- **✎ Edit** opens right where you tapped, in a pop-up, instead of at the bottom of the page. While you're in there, **✎⋮ Stages…** takes you straight to the steps.
- **Drag stages by their ⋮⋮ handle** to reorder them. The little ▲▼ still work for nudging one step.
- **＋ Save as a new template…**, at the bottom of a project's stages, turns the steps you've just shaped into a pipeline for next time.

### 5. The clock

If you need to know where your hours went, you can clock in to a project and clock out again, and the app keeps a ledger. **▶ Clock in** is on each project's row in Today as well as on its card, so you can start the clock from wherever you are. There's a running timer at the top of the list while it's going, and a Time Report that totals it up.

**At most one clock runs at a time.** Clocking in to something else closes what was open, in the same instant, so the ledger can never claim you were doing two things at once.

## Views

Four, along the top:

- **Dashboard** — where you probably live. Today's queue plus what's coming.
- **Day** — one day at a time, including days that aren't today.
- **Week** — the shape of the week. Where you notice that Thursday is a disaster while there's still time to do something about it.
- **Year** — the long view, for projects and deadlines months out. The tier buttons above it hide a whole tier's projects from the calendar — laundry out of the way while you shuffle work — without hiding them from your to-do list.

Which view you're on is remembered per device, so your phone and your laptop can disagree and both be right.

## Sharing

Two sizes, and picking the right one matters more than it looks.

### Share a whole board — ⚙️ ▸ People

You hand somebody a key to your board. They see **everything**: your tiers, your tasks, your projects, your calendars. They switch to it from the chip in the header, and the chip goes dashed to remind them they're a guest in your house rather than in their own.

Four levels of key:

| | Can they… |
|---|---|
| **Co-owner** | everything, including handing out keys |
| **Can edit** | tasks, projects, tiers, settings, calendar wiring |
| **Can help** | tasks, projects and the clock — **not** your tiers or settings |
| **Can view** | look, and cheer. Nothing else. |

**"Can help" is the one to reach for** when a colleague needs to see where you're at and tick off the thing they helped with. "Can edit" would also let them repoint your board at a different Google Calendar, which is almost certainly not what you meant.

### Share one tier — ⚙️ ▸ Tiers ▸ 🤝

The tier, its tasks, its projects and their clocked hours move onto a small board you both hold, and it then appears **inside both of your queues** next to your own tiers. Nobody switches anywhere. Neither of you sees anything else of the other's.

This is usually what you want. Without it, the day somebody shares a busy work board, eighty of their tasks interleave into your today list and you stop opening the app.

**It's the same documents, not copies.** They tick something off; it leaves your screen within a second.

And it's reversible: *Bring it back to my board* returns everything to you and the other person stops seeing it.

> **A tier you share follows you into a house where somebody who lives there also holds a key to it.** So the tier you share with your partner is still there when you're looking at their board — but the one you share with a colleague isn't, because it's nothing to do with them.

## Things that surprise people

**Checking something off doesn't hide it.** Completed items stay visible for a while, because a day you can see you finished is worth more than an empty screen.

**Nothing makes a sound during your sleep hours,** and hidden tiers stay silent even when they're overdue.

**Your view settings are yours, not the board's.** Visiting someone else's board, you keep your own layout and your own hidden tiers.

**Late things do not go away.** That's not a bug being tolerated, it's the product.

**On a day a tier doesn't run, its tasks don't nag.** A work task due next week stays out of your Saturday entirely. The one exception is something actually *dated* to that Saturday: it waits quietly in **Waiting on…** so you can still tick it off.

## When it's not doing what you expect

**Check the version first.** Hover the version number in the bottom corner — it lists every file and its version. If you've been told about a fix and the numbers look old, do a hard reload; you're probably running a cached copy.

**A tier you expected to see is missing?** It's probably hidden — check the tier chips — or it's set to days of the week that don't include today.

**Something's in the wrong place in the queue?** Look at its deadline. The queue is doing arithmetic, not having an opinion.
